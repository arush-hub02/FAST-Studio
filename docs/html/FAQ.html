Asked 2026-11-09

Can every quality level be configured per tenant from platform admin, and how do I see which level is playing in the video detail player?

Short answer. Yes. On the tenant page → Configuration → Quality levels, a platform administrator ticks any of 360p, 540p, 720p, 1080p, 1440p (2K) and 2160p (4K). That tenant's uploads and ads are converted to exactly those levels and its channels offer them. In the Library, the video's detail player now has a Quality menu (Auto or a fixed level) and a badge saying which level is playing.

1. Setting the levels (platform administrators)

  1. Platform administration → Tenants → the tenant → Configuration (scope: Tenant defaults).
  2. Tick the levels in Quality levels; the card shows the storage per hour of video at your choice (e.g. about 3.9 GB for 720p + 1080p, about 8 GB for 4K alone).
  3. Save quality levels. New conversions use them within 30 seconds.

Only platform administrators can change this (a tenant owner gets "Only the platform administrator may change this setting"), because higher levels cost encoding time, storage and delivery. The default is 360p, 540p and 720p. It applies to the whole tenant, every project.

2. What changes when you save

  • Uploads and ads converted after the change get exactly the chosen levels (one folder per level: 0 = 360p … 5 = 2160p) and an fMP4 copy of each. Existing videos are not converted again.
  • Channels offer the chosen levels. Where a video or ad lacks a level (converted before the change), that level plays its nearest lower one, so nothing breaks.
  • Slates carry all six levels, so they fit every tenant.
  • Mux videos keep the levels Mux made; each channel level plays the Mux level nearest in height. Uploads through FAST Studio ask Mux for the 1440p or 2160p tier when the tenant uses those levels (Mux charges more for them).
  • A small source (say 720p) is scaled up to every chosen level; it does not gain detail.

3. Seeing and choosing the level in the player

  1. Library → click a Ready video. Under the player: a Quality menu and a badge.
  2. Auto lets the player choose by connection speed; the menu shows which level Auto picked, e.g. "Auto (720p)".
  3. Pick a level (e.g. "1080p · 5.8 Mbit/s") to force it; the badge changes to e.g. "Playing 1080p · 1920×1080 · 5.8 Mbit/s" once the switch is done.
  4. "2 levels in this video" tells you how many levels the video actually has; for a Mux video these are Mux's levels.

4. Checked on 9 November 2026

On a separate production-mode copy: a tenant set to 720p + 1080p; a new video was converted to exactly those (folders 2 and 3, plus fMP4 copies); the player listed both and switched; a live channel offered "720p, 1080p" and its 720p and 1080p playlists used folders 2 and 3, every file downloadable.

Asked 2026-11-09

Do we support 4K or 2K?

Short answer. Not yet. FAST Studio plays at most 720p today, and your Mux video only has levels up to 1080p. Mux itself can make 2K/2.5K (1440p) and 4K (2160p) levels, but only for videos you upload with a higher resolution tier, which costs more. To play them, FAST Studio would first need 1080p, then 1440p and 2160p levels for its own ads and slate, or accept a lower picture during breaks.

1. What exists today

Highest level
FAST Studio (uploads, ads, slate, and what the live playlist offers)720p
Your Mux video "Songs Mashup 2010"1080p (two bitrates)
Mux in general, on-demand videoup to 2160p (4K), if the video is created with a higher max_resolution_tier

2. Mux and 2K/4K

  • Mux makes 1080p at most unless told otherwise: the default max_resolution_tier is 1080p, so a 4K source still becomes 1080p.
  • For 2K/2.5K set "max_resolution_tier": "1440p", for 4K "2160p", when the video is created or uploaded. FAST Studio's direct upload doesn't set it today; videos uploaded in the Mux dashboard can.
  • Higher tiers cost more for encoding, storage and delivery. It isn't clear that the free plan covers them; check Mux's pricing page before testing.

3. What FAST Studio would need

  1. A 1080p level first for ads, slate and uploads, so viewers get Mux's 1080p without the picture dropping at each break. Moderate work; more conversion time and storage per ad.
  2. For 2K/4K: 1440p/2160p levels for ads and slate as well, or let breaks play at 1080p inside a 4K channel (a visible quality drop at each break, but most ad creatives are 1080p anyway). 4K delivery is roughly 15–25 Mbit/s per viewer, so Mux delivery costs rise accordingly. 4K is often HEVC (H.265), which needs fMP4 (already supported) and players that can decode it; the live playlist would also have to declare HEVC.

Suggested order: 1080p now, 2K/4K only when a customer asks for it.

Asked 2026-11-09

What renditions does our platform support, and which did Mux give?

Short answer. FAST Studio makes and plays three quality levels: 360p, 540p and 720p. Mux gave your video "Songs Mashup 2010" five: 360p, 540p, 720p and two 1080p. Viewers get Mux's 360p, 540p and 720p (each of our levels takes the Mux level nearest in height); Mux's two 1080p levels are not used yet, because our ads and slate only go up to 720p.

1. FAST Studio's quality levels

Every video FAST Studio converts (uploads, generated samples, ads, slate) gets the same three levels, so playback can switch between programme, ad and slate at any 5-second piece:

LevelPictureVideo bitrateDeclared to players
360p640×360600 kbit/s0.8 Mbit/s
540p960×5401,400 kbit/s1.7 Mbit/s
720p1280×7202,500 kbit/s2.9 Mbit/s

All: H.264 (Main profile), 25 frames a second, a keyframe at every 5 s, AAC stereo audio at 96 kbit/s, loudness −23 LUFS. Each is stored as MPEG-TS and, since 9 November 2026, also as an fMP4 copy. Defined in MediaServiceV1.LADDER.

2. What Mux gave for your video

Read from production's database (table asset_playback) on 9 November 2026:

Mux levelPictureBitrate (Mux)Used by FAST Studio?
1080p1920×10803.9 Mbit/sNo
1080p1920×10802.9 Mbit/sNo
720p1280×7201.5 Mbit/sYes, for our 720p
540p960×5400.97 Mbit/sYes, for our 540p
360p640×3600.50 Mbit/sYes, for our 360p

Format fMP4 (CMAF), public playback (not signed). 291 pieces: 290 of exactly 5.000 s and a last one of 4.286 s, 24 min 14.3 s in total. In the schedule it takes 24 min 15 s (whole 5-second slots); the last 0.7 s are slate. Mux decides its levels from your source file (here a 1080p source), and adds a second, higher-bitrate 1080p level.

3. How the two are matched

  • The live playlist offers players our three levels. For each, the edge Worker takes the Mux level with the nearest height (on a tie, the lower one): 360p→360p, 540p→540p, 720p→720p.
  • During the programme the player gets Mux's pieces for that level; during breaks our ad and slate pieces of the same level, so the picture size stays the same across the join.
  • A different source gives different Mux levels (e.g. a 720p upload gives no 1080p). Matching by height still works; a channel never offers more than our 720p.

4. Things to know

  • No 1080p yet. To offer Mux's 1080p, FAST Studio must add a 1080p level for ads and slate (and uploads) first, otherwise the picture would drop at every break. Not built; listed as left in the test guide.
  • Declared bitrates are ours. The live playlist tells players our figures (e.g. 2.9 Mbit/s for 720p) while Mux's 720p is about 1.5 Mbit/s, so players on slow connections may pick a lower level than they could. Harmless, but could be tuned per channel later.
  • Codec profile. Ours is H.264 Main; Mux may use High. Players switch at each source change (there is a discontinuity marker), which web, iOS and Android TV players handle; still part of the real-device test.

Asked 2026-11-09

I synced Mux and my video got ready after "transcoding". Where is my video stored in the system, and how is it playing?

Short answer. Your Mux video is stored only in Mux. FAST Studio never downloads or converts it: "getting it ready" only reads its playlists and records how it is cut (quality levels and the length of every piece). When someone watches, our edge Worker writes a live playlist that sends the player to Mux for the programme and to our own storage (R2) for ads and slate.

MuxStores your video and serves its pieces (stream.mux.com / Mux CDN)
→
FAST Studio serverRemembers the video's details, the schedule and the ads; never serves video
→
Edge Worker (Cloudflare)Writes each viewer's live playlist every few seconds
→
R2 storage + CDNOur ads and slate, converted once by FFmpeg
→
PlayerDownloads programme pieces from Mux, ad and slate pieces from R2

1. What each step did

  1. Sync asked Mux for its list of videos and added each one to the Library as a Draft. Only a catalogue entry was created: title, length and a link to the Mux video (mux:<Mux asset id>).
  2. Get ready to stream (or Retry processing) is not a transcode for Mux videos, even though the screen used to call it that. FAST Studio asked Mux for the video's playlists and recorded: its playback ID, whether playback is signed, the container (fMP4 in your case), the quality levels (e.g. 360p, 720p) and the exact length of every ~5-second piece. That takes a few seconds and copies nothing. The video is then Ready.
  3. Your video's Delivery card shows exactly this: Streams from: Video library (not copied), the number of pieces, the quality levels and the format.

2. Where things are stored

WhatWhere
The video itself (every quality level, every piece)Mux only: Mux's storage, delivered by Mux's CDN. Nothing of it is in FAST Studio, R2 or on this PC.
Its catalogue entry (title, length, licence, ad break points)FAST Studio's database: table asset, plus asset_source (which Mux video it is and what Mux last said about it). On this PC the production database is in data-pilot\pg.
How it streams (playback ID, signed or not, fMP4/TS, quality levels, piece lengths)Same database, table asset_playback. No video addresses or tokens are stored; signed links are made when needed.
Your Mux access token and signing keySame database, encrypted (table connector_value). Never shown again after you save them.
Ads and slate ("We'll be right back", "Not available in your region")Converted once by FFmpeg into 5-second pieces (MPEG-TS and an fMP4 copy) in data-pilot\media, then copied to the edge storage: Cloudflare R2 in real hosting, data-pilot\edge on this PC (the local Worker stands in for R2).
Videos you upload straight to FAST Studio (not via Mux)Converted by FFmpeg like ads, in data-pilot\media and R2. Only these are stored by FAST Studio.
The schedule as the edge sees itA "snapshot" the server sends to the edge every few seconds when something changes (edge key-value storage; data-pilot\edge on this PC). For your Mux video it holds the Mux playlist address, the piece lengths and where each programme starts and ends.

3. How it plays, step by step

  1. You schedule the video on a channel, publish and press Go Live. The server writes the channel's snapshot to the edge.
  2. A viewer opens the channel (Watch live, VLC, Safari, a TV app). They get an address on the edge Worker, not on the FAST Studio server.
  3. Every few seconds the player asks the Worker for the live playlist. The Worker works it out from the snapshot and the clock: which programme or break is on now.
  4. For your Mux programme the Worker reads Mux's own playlist once per location and quality level (then reuses it for every viewer) and lists Mux's piece addresses at their real lengths.
  5. At an ad break it lists our pieces instead: ads chosen live for that viewer (your campaigns first, then ad servers such as Google Ad Manager), and slate for any time no ad fills. A marker (EXT-X-DISCONTINUITY) tells the player the source changes; for fMP4 each source also gets its own start header (EXT-X-MAP).
  6. The player downloads programme pieces directly from Mux and ad/slate pieces from R2 through Cloudflare's CDN. The FAST Studio server sends no video at all; it only answers "which ads for this break".
  7. If Mux can't be reached, the Worker plays slate for the programme's length and returns to Mux when it can; the channel never stops.

4. See it for yourself

  • Library → your video → Delivery card: "Video library (not copied)", pieces, quality levels, fMP4.
  • Watch live → Live HLS Manifest Inspector: the playlist the player gets. During the programme the addresses point at Mux; during breaks at the edge (…/media/…), with EXT-X-MAP headers for fMP4.
  • docs/html/visualizeDB.html: the tables asset, asset_source and asset_playback.
  • Folder data-pilot\media: you will find the slates and any ads or uploads, but no folder for your Mux video.

5. What it means for cost

Mux bills for viewing minutes of your programmes (its free plan covers 100,000 minutes a month). Ads and slate come from R2, which charges nothing for data sent to viewers. Keeping Mux videos out of R2 was your decision on 3 November 2026 (docs/OVP_DELIVERY_PLAN.md).
